# Writing modes

`plain` supports replies, plans, handoffs, emails, and general prose. Keep the user's tone and meaning. Sentence budgets guide the review. Do not claim standard vocabulary coverage.

`technical` supports procedures and technical descriptions. The checker treats the configured sentence budgets as errors. Use clear action sentences in procedures. Split sequential instructions only when the split keeps the action order and conditions. Keep simultaneous actions together when the procedure needs them.

`reference-review` adds targeted lookups in an authorized local standard. It uses the same structural checks as technical mode. The CLI has no reference-review mode because a script cannot make source-based semantic rulings.

Read a company glossary as local guidance. Its term roles do not certify standard approval. Keep a technical noun when a reader needs it. Review a possible role mismatch in context. Do not replace words from a universal substitution table.

Report a missing actor or other material ambiguity. Do not supply an invented fact to make the prose look simpler.

For technical reviews, use a coverage statement such as: “Technical review. Count method: ste100-project-count-v1. Vocabulary and meaning need review.” Add the established reference issue and physical PDF pages when a source ruling uses them.
